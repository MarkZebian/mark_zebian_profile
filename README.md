# Mark Zebian Engineering

Create a modern, highly polished personal portfolio website for Mark Zebian, a third-year Mechatronics Engineering student at Ontario Tech University, focused on robotics, automation, AI, embedded systems, and intelligent technology.

The website should communicate one central idea:

An engineer who enjoys turning ideas into things that can actually be built.

Mark is currently a student and is not offering freelance or business services, so the website should not present him as a consultant or business owner. Instead, position him as an aspiring engineer actively developing his technical skills and looking for a co-op opportunity in robotics, automation, embedded systems, or intelligent technology.

1. Overall Visual Direction

The website should feel technical, modern, intelligent, and engineering-focused, while still being approachable and professional.

Avoid the appearance of a generic university student template. The design should feel closer to the personal website of a young robotics/mechatronics engineer.

Use:

Clean modern typography
Strong visual hierarchy
Smooth scrolling
Subtle animations
Interactive hover effects
Technical visual details
Geometric shapes
Subtle grid patterns
Circuit-board or robotics-inspired visual motifs
Clean cards for skills and certifications
Responsive design for desktop, tablet, and mobile

Do not overcrowd the site with unnecessary animations. The technology-inspired elements should enhance the content rather than distract from it.

Visual Theme Option A: Robotic Engineering

Use a sophisticated robotic/industrial palette:

Deep charcoal / near-black
White
Light grey
Metallic silver
Dark grey
Gold accents
Small amounts of red for emphasis

The gold should feel metallic and premium rather than bright yellow. Red should be used sparingly for buttons, indicators, highlights, or small interface details.

The visual language can be inspired by:

Robotics
Industrial machinery
Mechanical components
Control panels
CAD interfaces
Sensors
Circuit boards
Engineering schematics
Visual Theme Option B: Ontario Tech Inspired

Alternatively, create a clean theme inspired by Ontario Tech:

Orange as the primary accent
White
Light blue
Dark navy/charcoal for text and contrast
Light grey backgrounds

This version should feel energetic, academic, technological, and connected to engineering without looking like an official Ontario Tech website.

Important: Do not combine both colour palettes heavily. Choose either the robotic red/white/grey/silver/gold direction or the Ontario Tech orange/white/light-blue direction and maintain strong visual consistency throughout the website.

15. Tone & Copywriting

The writing should be:

Professional
Intelligent
Genuine
Curious
Hands-on
Ambitious without exaggeration

Avoid generic phrases like:

"I'm a passionate rockstar"
"I deliver cutting-edge solutions"
"I'm a visionary leader"
"I transform businesses"

Mark is a student, so the website should embrace that.

The message should instead communicate:

I'm learning, I'm building, I'm curious, and I'm ready to contribute.

16. Important Accuracy Requirements

Do not invent:

Work experience
Engineering employment
Co-op experience
Projects
Clients
Freelance services
Professional achievements
Technical expertise that Mark did not provide

Clearly distinguish between:

Certified skills, technical skills, and areas of interest.

Mark currently has no formal engineering work experience and no notable completed projects to showcase, so the website should present his portfolio as an emerging engineering portfolio.

The goal is to make the website impressive because of its design, technical identity, education, skills, certifications, and potential, not because of exaggerated experience.

Overall Impression

The finished website should make a recruiter think:

"This is a technically curious Mechatronics Engineering student who understands how different engineering disciplines come together, has a solid technical foundation, and is actively looking for an opportunity to apply what he's learning."

It should feel like the beginning of an engineering career, not a finished corporate résumé.

The visual identity should combine robotics + engineering + technology + personal identity, with either the red/white/grey/silver/gold robotic aesthetic or the orange/white/light-blue Ontario Tech-inspired aesthetic.

The final design should be modern, polished, memorable, technically sophisticated, and easy for an engineering recruiter to scan in under a minute.
